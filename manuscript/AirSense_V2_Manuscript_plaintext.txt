Abstract

Fine particulate matter is a leading environmental health burden in dense urban
areas, and short-range forecasts underpin public advisories. Reported
air-quality forecasting results are dominated by ordinary conditions, so a model
can post a strong average score while failing during the episodes that motivate
forecasting at all. This study asks how much a learned model gains over a naive
causal reference across forecast horizons, and whether any of that gain reaches
the extreme tail.

We forecast PM2.5 at twelve monitoring stations of the Beijing Multi-Site
Air-Quality network over 2013-03-01 to 2017-02-28, comprising 420,768 hourly
records. Data are partitioned into contiguous chronological blocks by target
timestamp, and the final block is sealed. Every input obeys a strict causal
rule: no value may postdate its forecast origin, and future observed
meteorology and pollutants are prohibited by construction. Models observe a
48-hour context and predict at 1, 6, 12 and 24 hours. Four model families were
developed — engineered-feature gradient boosting, recurrent and convolutional
sequence models, an inverted-attention transformer variant, and a
station-attention model — under nested information regimes. Model roles were
frozen before the sealed block was opened; it was opened once and scored once,
over 411,012 samples.

The primary confirmatory model achieved a macro station-horizon mean absolute
error of 31.99 µg/m³ against 36.14 for causal persistence, an improvement of
11.49%, and it improved on persistence at every horizon from 1 to 24 hours. In
the severe stratum, defined as observed PM2.5 above the training pooled P95 of
244.0 µg/m³ (n = 20,336), the prespecified secondary model reached 109.33 µg/m³
while persistence remained the strongest severe reference benchmark at 93.22.
Average multi-horizon accuracy and extreme-episode accuracy therefore diverge:
the improvement over persistence is real on the bulk distribution and does not
extend to the tail, which remains the principal open problem.

1. Introduction

1.1 Why short-range PM2.5 forecasting matters

Fine particulate matter with aerodynamic diameter below 2.5 µm penetrates deep
into the respiratory tract and is associated with cardiovascular and
respiratory morbidity in dense urban populations [REF-NEEDED: authoritative
review or cohort study establishing PM2.5 health associations, with effect
sizes]. Operational value depends on lead time: an advisory issued an hour
ahead supports different decisions from one issued a day ahead. That makes
PM2.5 forecasting intrinsically multi-horizon rather than a single-step
problem.

1.2 Why multi-horizon forecasting is hard

Forecast difficulty is not uniform in lead time. Observed PM2.5 is strongly
autocorrelated at short lags and much less so at a full day's lead, so the
informational content of recent history decays sharply across exactly the
range where forecasts are useful. A model tuned to the horizon where
persistence is nearly unbeatable can look excellent overall while adding
nothing where the forecast is actually needed.

1.3 Why average accuracy is insufficient

Aggregate error metrics are dominated by ordinary hours. Severe episodes are
rare by construction, so a model that regresses toward the conditional mean
during extremes can still minimise mean absolute error overall. Any claim that
a method improves air-quality forecasting must therefore state which part of
the distribution improved, and a study that reports only an average has not
answered the question that motivates the field.

1.4 Why severe episodes are scientifically important

Severe pollution hours carry the health burden and trigger public response.
They are also the regime where the physical processes driving concentration —
regional accumulation, boundary-layer collapse, meteorological stagnation —
differ most from ordinary conditions. A forecasting system evaluated only on
its average behaviour leaves the scientifically distinctive regime untested.

1.5 Why history, co-pollutants and cross-station context are reasonable dimensions

Three sources of information plausibly add value beyond meteorology: the
target station's own pollutant history, which carries the autocorrelation
structure; co-pollutant history, which may index shared emission and
accumulation processes; and observations from other stations in the network,
which may carry regional structure not visible at a single site. These are
distinguishable, so they can be studied as nested information regimes rather
than conflated into one model.

1.6 Why chronological generalisation and leakage control matter

Air-quality series are strongly autocorrelated, so a randomly partitioned
evaluation places near-duplicate hours on both sides of the split and reports
an optimistic number. Contiguous chronological partitioning is the minimum
defensible design. Beyond partitioning, the subtler risk is that a preprocessing
statistic, an imputation rule or a smoothing window silently consults data
after the forecast origin. Leakage of that kind does not announce itself in the
metric; it merely improves it.

1.7 Why foundation-model eligibility affects valid evaluation

Pretrained time-series foundation models are trained on large heterogeneous
corpora whose contents are often enumerated only at dataset granularity. If a
pretraining corpus overlaps the evaluation period and network, a held-out test
is no longer held out for that model, and the resulting comparison measures
recall as much as generalisation. Licensing imposes a second, independent
eligibility constraint. Both are governance questions that must be settled
before a checkpoint is run, not after.

1.8 Research question

Under strict causal, chronologically partitioned evaluation on a sealed test
block opened exactly once: how much does a learned model improve on a naive
causal reference across forecast horizons of 1 to 24 hours, and does that
improvement extend to severe pollution episodes?

1.9 Hypotheses

Five hypotheses were recorded before the corresponding experiments. They are
reproduced here in their frozen form; their evidence status is reported in
Development-validation results, Robustness results and Limitations, and
the reader should note in advance that none was re-tested as a paired contrast
on the locked final test.

- H1. Adding target-station PM2.5 history improves macro station-horizon
  mean absolute error.
- H2. The benefit of target PM2.5 history is largest at short horizons and
  decreases as lead time grows.
- H3. Adding co-pollutant history provides additional predictive value.
- H4. A cross-station information regime improves on the same architecture
  without it.
- H5. Modern sequence and cross-station methods reduce severe-tail error
  and under-prediction relative to engineered and naive baselines.

1.10 Contributions

We state these factually; none is claimed as unprecedented.

1. A leakage-controlled multi-horizon PM2.5 forecasting framework. Sample
   eligibility, imputation, scaling and calendar encoding are defined so that
   every input timestamp is at or before the forecast origin, with the
   constraint enforced structurally rather than by convention.
2. An information-regime study. Target history, co-pollutant history and
   cross-station context are separated into nested regimes evaluated over a
   common sample universe, so their contributions are distinguishable.
3. A controlled paired cross-station experiment with multi-seed
   robustness. One architecture is run with cross-station access enabled and
   disabled, holding capacity and training protocol fixed, replicated across
   five seeds.
4. A locked final-test protocol with a predeclared confirmatory hierarchy.
   Primary, secondary and reference roles were frozen before the test block was
   opened; predictions were generated and hashed before any metric was
   computed; the block was opened once.
5. A severe-tail analysis that reports a negative result. The reference
   persistence benchmark achieved the lowest severe error of the three
   evaluated models, and this is reported rather than reframed.
6. A foundation-model eligibility audit. Pretraining-overlap and licensing
   constraints were assessed before any checkpoint was obtained, and the
   resulting exclusions are documented as a governance outcome.
7. An evidence-governance framework. Every quantitative claim in this
   manuscript carries an evidence class and traces to a hash-pinned artifact.

3. Data and protocol

3.1 Dataset

We use the Beijing Multi-Site Air-Quality dataset (UCI ID 501,
doi:10.24432/C5RK5G), distributed under CC BY 4.0 as presented by UCI. It
comprises hourly observations from twelve monitoring stations — Aotizhongxin,
Changping, Dingling, Dongsi, Guanyuan, Gucheng, Huairou, Nongzhanguan, Shunyi,
Tiantan, Wanliu and Wanshouxigong — over 2013-03-01 to 2017-02-28, giving
420,768 station-hours. Variables are PM2.5, PM10, SO2, NO2, CO, O3,
temperature, pressure, dew point, rain, wind speed and categorical wind
direction. The station order above is frozen and used consistently throughout.

Every source CSV is hash-pinned, and the raw archive digest is recorded, so the
input to the pipeline is verifiable. No value was cleaned, corrected or
imputed at source. (C5)

3.2 Chronological partitions

Partitions are contiguous chronological blocks assigned by target
timestamp, not by forecast origin. Table 1 gives the design.

    Partition          Target window             Eligible samples  Status
    -----------------  ------------------------  ----------------  -------------------
    Train              2013-03-01 to 2015-02-28  821,184           open throughout
    Validation         2015-03-01 to 2016-02-29  413,148           development only
    Locked final test  2016-03-01 to 2017-02-28  411,012           sealed, opened once


Table 1. Dataset and chronological split. (C5)

Partitioning by target timestamp has a consequence worth stating: a target
early in the validation or test window legitimately has a forecast origin, and
therefore context, inside the preceding partition. Those samples are kept. A
forecaster operating on the first day of a new period does have the previous
day's observations, and using them is rolling-origin history rather than
leakage. What makes this safe is that model parameters and every preprocessing
statistic remain fitted on training data alone; newly observed past context may
enter an input window, but nothing is refitted from it. (C5)

3.3 Forecast task and severe stratum

A sample is the triple (station, target timestamp, horizon). Models observe a
48-hour context ending at the forecast origin and predict PM2.5 at 1, 6, 12 and
24 hours ahead, giving 48 station-horizon cells. A sample is eligible only if
PM2.5 at the target timestamp is observed in the raw data; targets are never
imputed.

The severe stratum is defined as observed PM2.5 > 244.0 µg/m³. That value
is the training pooled P95 — a study-internal quantile computed on training
data only. It is not a regulatory limit and not an air-quality-index
boundary, and it should not be read as one. The rule is strictly greater than:
on the locked final test 232 targets sit at exactly 244.0 µg/m³ and are
excluded from the severe stratum, so a non-strict rule would define a larger
severe set than the 20,336 samples reported here. The training pooled median, 61.0 µg/m³, bounds the exploratory
concentration strata used later. (C5)

3.4 Preprocessing

Every rule distinguishes what it is fitted on from what it is applied
to. Statistics are fitted on training data only and applied unchanged to all
partitions. (C5)

- Targets are never imputed. A sample with an unobserved target is
  excluded, not filled.
- Causal forward fill with a six-hour ceiling. A missing input is carried
  from the most recent observation at or before the origin, up to six hours.
  The ceiling is the rounded P90 gap length and is where training
  autocorrelation stops supporting the carry.
- Training-median fallback. Beyond six hours the value is replaced by the
  station's training median and marked as not observed. Long outages are not
  dropped, because outages concentrate by station and dropping them would bias
  the macro metric unevenly across sites.
- Missingness masks and gap age. Every imputed variable carries a binary
  observed mask computed from the raw value before any fill, plus a
  time-since-last-observation channel capped at 168 hours, so a one-hour-stale
  value is distinguishable from a sixty-day-stale one.
- Robust scaling. Inputs are scaled as (x − median) / IQR with constants
  pooled across stations over the training partition. Robust rather than
  standard scaling, because training PM2.5 is right-skewed and a
  standard-deviation scale is dominated by the tail. Scaling is global rather
  than per-station, deliberately: station level differences are the signal the
  macro metric exists to compare.
- Rain exception. RAIN has a zero interquartile range in training, so its
  robust denominator is undefined; it falls back to a standard-deviation scale.
- Wind direction is treated categorically and never interpolated
  numerically, with a 17-way encoding of the sixteen training compass
  categories plus an explicit missing state.
- No target transform. PM2.5 remains in native µg/m³ so that mean absolute
  error is directly interpretable. No log transform was applied.
- Prohibited outright: backward fill, any interpolation consulting
  observations after the origin, centred rolling statistics, any bidirectional
  transform, and any statistic fitted on validation or test data.

3.5 Information regimes

Models are compared under nested information regimes over a common sample
universe, so that a difference between regimes is a difference in information
rather than in data. Table 2 defines them.

    Regime  Available information
    ------  ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
    R0      Meteorology, wind direction, rain occurrence, calendar, masks. No pollutant history.
    R1      R0 plus target-station PM2.5 history, its mask and its gap age.
    R2      R1 plus co-pollutant history (PM10, SO2, NO2, CO, O3) with masks and gap ages.
    R3      R2 plus cross-station history.


Table 2. Information regimes. (C5)

The causality rules are absolute and identical in every regime:

- future observed meteorology — prohibited;
- future observed pollutants — prohibited;
- deterministic future calendar — permitted, because it is knowable at the
  origin without observing anything;
- historical observations — only timestamps at or before the forecast origin;
- rolling PM2.5 reveal — permitted only once a timestamp has become past
  relative to the forecast origin.

Enforcement is structural, not advisory. Across 1,233,036 index checks during
final-test prediction generation there were 0 future-target access
violations. (C5)


4. Models

Four families were developed, plus three naive references. Only three models
carried a role on the locked final test; the rest are development-only and are
reported as such. No foundation model was executed.

4.1 Naive reference models

- B0 — causal persistence. Predict the latest usable PM2.5 observation at
  the target station available at the forecast origin, resolving in order:
  the value observed at the origin; otherwise the most recent observation
  within the six-hour ceiling; otherwise the station training median. The
  fallback guarantees a prediction for every eligible sample, so B0 cannot
  improve its score by declining hard cases.
- B1 — daily seasonal naive. Predict the value observed 24 hours before
  the target, used only when that observation is causally available at the
  origin, otherwise falling back to B0. At a 24-hour horizon the seasonal lag
  coincides with the origin, so B1 collapses exactly into B0. This is
  arithmetic, and it is reported rather than presented as an independent
  reference.
- B2 — training climatology. A training-only median indexed by the target
  timestamp's calendar position, with a deterministic fallback from
  station-month-hour to station-hour to station. It uses no observation at all
  and is horizon-independent by construction.

Defining the references before building the learned models is deliberate: it
prevents a sophisticated model being credited for work a one-line rule already
does.

4.2 B3 — gradient-boosted causal lag features

B3 is gradient boosting (LightGBM, native Booster API) over engineered causal
features derived from the 48-hour context, trained per horizon.

Features, frozen before any validation metric existed: lag positions 0, 1,
2, 3, 6, 12, 24 and 47 hours before the origin; trailing windows of 3, 6, 12,
24 and 48 hours ending at the origin with mean, standard deviation, minimum and
maximum; observed mask at origin plus observed fraction over 6, 12 and 48
hours; gap age at origin for each pollutant in the regime; a rain-occurred
indicator and its trailing means; a 17-way wind encoding; a 12-way station
encoding; and frozen origin and target cyclic calendar channels. Day-of-week
and weekend channels are excluded, because training autocorrelation at a
168-hour lag gives no support for a weekly cycle.

Lag 48 is deliberately excluded. The canonical window spans exactly 48
timestamps ending at the origin, so an exact 48-hour lag would sit one hour
outside it; honouring it would mean quietly reading a 49th hour.

Feature counts are 207, 240 and 405 for R0, R1 and R2 respectively.

Hyperparameters were selected from exactly eight predeclared candidates —
learning rate in {0.03, 0.07}, leaves in {31, 63}, minimum data per leaf in
{50, 200} — with 400 boosting rounds fixed, L1 objective, L2 regularisation
1.0, no bagging or feature subsampling, and no early stopping, because an
early-stopped iteration count is one more quantity chosen by looking at
validation. Selection used macro-station validation MAE at that horizon with a
frozen tie-break order. Determinism was pinned: all seeds 42, deterministic and
column-wise modes enabled, thread count fixed at 16 across every candidate.

4.3 GRU and TCN sequence models

Both are trained jointly across horizons, with horizon supplied as a four-way
one-hot static feature, rather than one model per horizon. Each canonical
sample still receives exactly one prediction and metrics are still reported per
horizon over the same 48 cells.

- GRU. A single-layer unidirectional GRU over the 48-hour window, final
  hidden state concatenated with the static vector, followed by a
  linear-ReLU-dropout-linear head.
- TCN. Four residual blocks of two kernel-3 causal convolutions with
  dilations 1, 2, 4 and 8 and left padding only. The receptive field is 61
  timesteps, covering the whole context; causality is asserted both by formula
  and by a test that perturbs future inputs and requires bit-identical output
  at earlier positions.

Both heads are unconstrained — no ReLU, softplus or clipping — so negative
predictions remain visible rather than being silently hidden.

Dynamic input channels number 28, 31 and 46 for R0, R1 and R2; the static
vector has 28 channels. Hyperparameters were selected on a training-only
internal split: a fit block of 410,696 samples over 2013-03-01 to
2014-02-28 and a scoring block of 410,488 samples over 2014-03-01 to
2015-02-28. Both are complete annual cycles, the scoring block is strictly
later, and together they partition the training universe exactly. This matters
because the development validation partition had already been spent selecting
classical candidates; tuning every later architecture on the same year would
compound selection optimism until that partition stopped being informative.

Four candidates per family were scored — GRU hidden size in {64, 128}, TCN
channels in {32, 64}, learning rate in {3e-4, 1e-3} — with everything else
fixed: 8 epochs, batch 1024, AdamW, weight decay 1e-4, gradient clipping at
1.0, L1 loss, head width 64, dropout 0.1, seed 42, no scheduler, no warmup, no
early stopping. The selected configurations were GRU hidden 64 at 3e-4 and TCN
channels 32 at 1e-3.

4.4 iTransformer-style model

An in-repository iTransformer-style architecture, not an official reference
implementation: attention is applied across variates rather than time steps,
with 2 encoder layers, 4 heads, feedforward ratio 4, GELU activation, layer
normalisation, dropout 0.1 and no positional encoding across variates. The
selected configuration was model width 64 at learning rate 1e-3, under R1 and
R2 only. It is not a foundation model and is not described as one.

4.5 Station-attention model

The cross-station model is an AirSense Station-Attention Forecaster, and it
is explicitly not an implementation of any published graph forecasting
architecture. A shared unidirectional GRU encodes each station's context; a
single 4-head attention layer then operates along the station axis, fused
by layer normalisation. There is no graph convolution, no distance mask, no
chosen neighbour set and no coordinate input.

The experiment is a paired contrast holding architecture, capacity,
training protocol and seed fixed, varying only information access:

- SA_R2 — self-only attention: every off-diagonal station edge is
  forbidden, so a station may attend only to itself.
- SA_R3 — unmasked attention: all twelve stations are reachable.

The selected configuration was hidden size 128 at learning rate 1e-3, 144,001
parameters, grouped batch size 512.

4.6 Foundation-model eligibility

No foundation model was executed, and no foundation-model prediction exists.
This is a governance outcome, not a measurement, and it must not be read as a
performance comparison.

- Chronos-2 was excluded before any checkpoint was obtained. Its published
  pretraining corpus enumerates a dataset whose Beijing air-quality content
  overlaps the sealed evaluation period and network, which under the study's

  provenance; it did not demonstrate that any specific record entered

- TimesFM-3.0 was excluded because its weight licence is incompatible with

- Moirai-2.0-R-small was excluded because its weights carry a

5. Evaluation metrics

Primary metric — macro station-horizon MAE. Mean absolute error is computed
within each of the 48 station-horizon cells and averaged with equal weight.
Equal weighting is the point: it prevents high-volume or high-variance sites
from dominating, and it makes the twelve stations comparable. This metric was
fixed before any model was built and was never replaced.

Secondary and descriptive metrics. Micro MAE over pooled samples, macro and
micro RMSE, and coefficient of determination are reported as descriptive
context. Micro MAE was never used for model selection.

Severe metrics. Restricted to samples with observed PM2.5 > 244.0 µg/m³,
reported as severe MAE, severe RMSE, severe mean residual and severe
under-prediction rate.

Residual convention. Residual = actual − prediction. A positive residual
therefore denotes under-prediction, and this sign convention is used without
exception throughout the manuscript.

No inferential procedure is reported. None was predeclared before the test was
opened, so no interval, no p-value and no significance test appears anywhere in
this study. Differences are reported as measured quantities. (C5)


6. Experimental protocol

Evidence was accumulated in stages, and the stage at which a result was
produced determines its evidence class.

Stage 1 — training-only audit and preprocessing. Exploratory analysis,
missingness characterisation and every preprocessing constant were derived from
training data alone. The test partition was structurally unreadable: the data
loader refused to return values for any partition other than training except in
a presence-checking mode that discards numeric values in the parser.

Stage 2 — development validation. Classical references and engineered
gradient boosting were evaluated on the validation partition.

Stage 3 — architecture and regime evaluation. Sequence, transformer-style
and station-attention families were added, with hyperparameters selected on the
training-only internal split rather than on validation.

Stage 4 — robustness and controlled cross-station analysis. Candidates were
replicated across five seeds; the paired cross-station contrast was replicated;
leave-one-station-out transfer and attention diagnostics were run.

Stage 5 — pre-test role freeze. Primary, secondary and reference roles,
endpoints and the severe threshold were frozen in a signed artifact while the
test remained sealed.

Stage 6 — single locked final-test evaluation. An authorisation receipt was
written; predictions were generated chronologically with metrics unseen;
prediction arrays were hashed and locked; only then were metrics computed.

Stage 7 — post-test exploratory diagnostics. Error analysis was performed
from the frozen prediction arrays, after scoring, and is reported separately as
exploratory.

The governance mechanics that enforce these stages — per-phase validators,
append-only tooling registries, manifest hashing — are summarised in
Reproducibility statement and detailed in manuscript/SUPPLEMENTARY_METHODS.md.

7.1 Target history dominates the gain (H1)

Moving from R0 to R1 in the boosted family reduced macro MAE from 40.62 to
30.47. Meteorology and calendar alone (R0, 40.62) are worse than simply
carrying the last observation forward (B0, 33.13). Almost everything the
boosted model gains over persistence comes from PM2.5's own history, not from
meteorology. H1 is supported on development validation. It was not
re-tested as a paired contrast on the locked final test, because neither B3_R0
nor B3_R1 was an authorised final-test model. (C2)

7.2 The gain is largest at short lead (H2)

On development validation the R0-to-R1 benefit was largest at the shortest
horizon and diminished with lead time, consistent with the decay of PM2.5
autocorrelation. H2 is supported on development validation and was not
re-tested on the locked final test. (C2)

7.3 Co-pollutant history is model-dependent (H3)

Adding co-pollutant history (R1 to R2) changed macro MAE by +0.36 for the
boosted family — an improvement — but by −0.93 for the GRU, −3.39 for the TCN
and −0.24 for the transformer variant, where a negative value is a
degradation. The direction of the effect depends on the model family: it helped
only gradient boosting. H3 has weak, model-dependent development support,
and the general claim that co-pollutants improve forecasting is not supported.
(C2)

7.4 Architectures cluster more tightly than families suggest

Four of the learned configurations land between 30.11 and 30.91 on macro MAE. A
learned sequence representation did not separate itself from engineered lag
features on this task at development scale. The severe tail behaves quite
differently: the GRU at R1 reached 118.96 against the boosted model's 136.61,
while persistence stayed ahead of both at 101.27. This divergence between
overall and severe behaviour is what motivated a secondary severe-tail role in
the frozen hierarchy.

8.1 Severe-tail metrics are far less seed-stable than overall metrics

Overall macro MAE varies across seeds by roughly 0.3 to 1.4 µg/m³, while severe

severe-tail claims should be treated as provisional, including several made
earlier in this programme. Second, the apparent rank order among the learned
models on overall MAE sits inside seed noise: GRU_R1 and TCN_R1 have mean macro
MAE of 30.76 and 30.54 respectively, while TCN_R1 alone spans 0.80 across its
five seeds.

Reseeding also corrected an earlier published characterisation. TCN_R1's
severe-tail weakness, reported from a single run at 152.53, spans 123.10 to
152.53 across seeds with a mean of 137.76 — close to the boosted model's
136.61. The published figure was that model's worst of five.

8.2 The paired cross-station contrast (H4)

SA_R2 and SA_R3 differ only in information access: identical architecture,
capacity, training protocol and seed, with cross-station attention forbidden or
permitted. Across five paired seeds, SA_R3 was better than SA_R2 on overall
macro MAE in 5 of 5 seeds. At the published seed the improvement was 32.24 to
31.53, or 2.19%. Across seeds the means were 32.52 for SA_R2 and 31.55 for
SA_R3, and in the severe tail 154.44 against 136.82, with SA_R3 better in 5 of
5 seeds there as well.

The horizon structure is not uniform. Mean paired differences, where a positive
value favours cross-station access, were 0.19 at 1 hour, 1.99 at 6 hours, 1.54
at 12 hours and 0.17 at 24 hours. At 6 and 12 hours all five seeds agreed in
direction; at 1 and 24 hours they did not. An earlier single-seed finding that
cross-station context was harmful at 24 hours did not survive reseeding, and
the honest statement is that cross-station information neither reliably helps
nor reliably hurts at a full day's lead.

H4 is robustly supported on development and robustness evidence, and was not
independently confirmed on the locked final test, because SA_R2 and SA_R3
were excluded from the confirmatory set. (C2)

8.3 Attention is not proximity-driven

Station coordinates were acquired after every model was frozen, from a
licensed, citable source, and verified against it. They were used for
descriptive diagnostics only: never a model input, never a graph, never a
selection criterion.

Across 132 directed station pairs, the Spearman correlation between
inter-station distance and learned attention weight is +0.136289 — slightly
positive, meaning attention very mildly increases with distance. The nearest
station is the most-attended source for 1 of 12 stations. By contrast, the
Spearman correlation between distance and training PM2.5 correlation is
−0.938086, confirming that the coordinates are correctly mapped and that nearer
stations genuinely are more correlated. The absence of a distance effect in
attention is therefore a finding rather than a mapping error.

Cross-station access improves this architecture, and the mechanism is not
proximity. These weights must not be interpreted as physical transport. (C2)

8.4 Leave-one-station-out transfer

Twelve folds each withheld one station's training targets from the loss while
leaving that station's causal history visible to the shared encoder, then
evaluated on exactly that station. The mean relative penalty was +0.134022%
— a mean absolute penalty of 0.015185 µg/m³ — and 5 of 12 stations were better
when held out. The severe tail cost slightly more, at 3.394045 µg/m³ mean.

The required description of this experiment is held-out target-station
supervision transfer with causal historical observations from the held-out
station available. It is not forecasting at a site whose observations are
absent: only the station's training targets were withheld, while its
observation stream still reached the shared encoder. (C2)

9.3 The reference benchmark held the severe tail

The causal persistence reference benchmark B0 recorded the lowest severe MAE

the endpoint that motivates air-quality forecasting most directly, neither
learned model improved on carrying the last observation forward.

promoted to any other role by this outcome. Equally, GRU_R1's lower severe MAE

reported, and no overall winner label is assigned. A single label would collapse
two endpoints that behave differently — the primary model leads overall while
the reference benchmark leads in the severe tail.

9.4 What the two endpoints mean together

Both halves belong in any summary of this study. Engineered causal history beat
persistence on average across 1 to 24 hours, by 11.49%, and at every horizon.
Persistence remained the strongest severe benchmark. Reporting either half
alone misrepresents the result.

10.1 Generalisation from development to the locked test

All three models lost overall accuracy moving from development validation to
the locked test and gained severe-tail accuracy, the later period's tail being
somewhat easier. Relative ordering was preserved on both endpoints. Overall
degradation was smallest for B3_R2 at 6.23% and largest for B0 at 9.09%. (C3)

10.2 Horizon structure

Relative improvement over persistence for B3_R2 was 9.47% at 1 hour, 11.54% at
6 hours, 10.82% at 12 hours and 12.30% at 24 hours. GRU_R1 was markedly worse
than persistence at 1 hour, at −25.16%, and better at longer lead. Persistence
itself becomes weak in absolute terms at long lead: its micro R² at 24 hours is
−0.131365, meaning that at a full day's lead it is worse than predicting the
test mean — even though it still wins the severe tail. (C3)

10.3 Station structure

Northern suburban sites are easiest and central urban sites hardest for every
model, with near-identical ordering across models. For B3_R2 the easiest
station was Huairou at 26.835069 and the hardest Dongsi at 35.738812. Because
the ordering barely changes across models, it is associated with site character
rather than with model behaviour. (C3)

10.4 Residual temporal structure

Residual autocorrelation at lag 1 remains approximately 0.95 to 0.96 for every
model at a 24-hour horizon. Substantial short-lag residual dependence remains
after forecasting, indicating unresolved temporal structure in the locked
predictions. A large share of the remaining error is systematic structure
rather than noise.

At lag 24 the models separate by sign: +0.266948 for B3_R2, −0.232078 for
GRU_R1 and −0.297138 for B0. At lag 168 all three are near zero. This is an
association, and no causal mechanism is established by it. (C3)

10.5 Why persistence remains hard to beat in the tail

Observed PM2.5 is strongly autocorrelated: 0.971949 at 1 hour, 0.780487 at 6
hours and 0.427467 at 24 hours. At long horizons, persistence retains an
advantage during sustained severe episodes because recently observed extreme
values remain informative about a continuing episode. (C3)

10.6 Concentration regimes and regression toward the mean

Using strata bounded by training-derived constants, every model over-predicts
in the low stratum — mean residual −19.155200 for B3_R2, −22.121379 for GRU_R1
and −17.930293 for B0 — and under-predicts in the elevated and severe strata,
reaching mean residuals of 127.650421, 106.594564 and 65.549321 respectively in
the severe stratum. Error grows steeply with concentration.

The exploratory pattern is consistent with regression toward the conditional
mean during extreme episodes. Severe errors are strongly one-directional: the
mean residual accounts for most of the severe MAE, while residuals also retain
substantial temporal dependence. This is a description of the observed pattern,
not a demonstrated model mechanism, and no formal decomposition into bias and
variance components was performed — such a decomposition is only partially
identifiable from point forecasts. (C3)

10.7 The severe gap is a long-horizon phenomenon

Severe MAE by horizon shows the persistence advantage is concentrated at long
lead. At 1 hour the three models are close — 26.759515 for B3_R2, 26.016719 for
B0 and 56.010530 for GRU_R1. By 24 hours they diverge sharply: 229.790698 for
B3_R2, 152.630723 for GRU_R1 and 142.528324 for B0.

Severe under-prediction rises monotonically with horizon for every model,
reaching 99.901652% for B3_R2 at 24 hours, 96.144768% for GRU_R1 and 85.464201%
for B0. At a full day's lead the primary model under-predicts almost every
severe hour. (C3)

10.8 Severe events and detection

The test period contains 586 strict severe events, with a median duration of 4
hours. At 1 hour B3_R2 has a mean absolute error of 41.545912 at event peaks
and flags the first severe hour of 19.4539% of events. At 24 hours the mean
absolute error at peaks is 225.357642, the peak is under-predicted in 100.0% of
events, and the first severe hour is flagged in 0.3413%.

Severe detection recall at 24 hours is 0.006884 for B3_R2, 0.235641 for GRU_R1
and 0.356216 for B0, so persistence is also the best severe detector at long
lead.

The frozen severe-event diagnostics are insufficient to establish reliable
early-warning capability at 24 hours under the study's fixed 244.0 µg/m³
threshold. This is a statement about what the study measured. Operational
usability was never formally defined or tested here, so no claim is made in
either direction about fitness for operational use. (C3)

10.9 Seasonality and diurnal structure

Severe hours concentrate overwhelmingly in winter: at a 24-hour horizon the DJF
season holds 3,120 severe hours against 1,101 in MAM, 835 in SON and 28 in JJA.
Severe error is associated with the winter season. The diurnal pattern is
shallow by comparison, with error lowest in the morning and highest just after
midnight, spanning roughly 7 to 8 µg/m³ at a 24-hour horizon — small next to
the horizon and concentration effects. (C3)

10.10 Error complementarity

The three models largely fail on the same samples: absolute-error correlations
range from 0.667901 to 0.872808 overall. In the severe stratum, however, B0
holds the strictly smallest error on 52.876573% of samples, against 26.386703%
for B3_R2 and 20.736625% for GRU_R1. Persistence is therefore complementary to
the learned models in the tail.

This was measured descriptively. No ensemble, oracle or weighting scheme was
constructed, and no performance claim about any combination is made. (C3)

10.11 Negative predictions

Neither neural head is constrained to non-negative output, and nothing was
clipped. B3_R2 produced 53 negative predictions with a minimum of −14.424702;
GRU_R1 produced 1,358 with a minimum of −28.539839, concentrated at the 1-hour
horizon; B0 is structurally non-negative and produced 0, with a minimum
prediction of 2.0. Leaving these visible is deliberate: clipping would hide a
real property of the fitted models. (C3)

10.12 Structural continuity with the earlier V1 programme

A qualitative comparison with the earlier, frozen AirSense V1 programme is
recorded as C4. The two are different tasks on different periods with
different designs: V1 performed concurrent-hour PM2.5 estimation at a single
station with a different severe threshold, while V2 performs future
multi-horizon forecasting across twelve stations. No numerical benchmark
between them is valid, and none is computed or reported.

What carries over is structural. Two failure modes recur in both programmes:
systematic severe under-prediction, and strong residual temporal structure.
What V2 adds is a test the earlier programme could not run, because its task
had no persistence reference at all: the demonstration that persistence is hard
to beat during extreme episodes. The methodological progression is from
concurrent estimation to strict multi-horizon forecasting under a sealed
evaluation. (C4)


11. Discussion

Why the primary model generalises overall. B3_R2 carries an explicit causal
representation of recent pollutant, meteorological and temporal history. Its
advantage over persistence is consistent with richer causal history providing
useful information beyond the last observation. We do not attribute the
advantage to feature count: 405 features is a property of the R2 regime, not an
established cause of the result.

Why learned features beat persistence across the bulk distribution. Most
test hours are ordinary hours in which concentration is changing smoothly.
There, meteorological state, diurnal and seasonal position and multi-scale
trailing statistics all carry information that a single lagged value does not.
The 11.49% improvement is an average over a distribution dominated by such
hours.

Why severe episodes remain hard. During extreme episodes the conditional
distribution is far from its mean, and a model trained under an absolute-error
objective on a distribution whose 95th percentile is 244.0 µg/m³ has little
incentive to commit to extreme values. The exploratory evidence — one-directional
severe residuals, over-prediction at low concentrations and under-prediction at
high ones — is consistent with regression toward the conditional mean.

Why persistence is unusually strong during sustained extremes. Severe
episodes persist: the median event lasts 4 hours and observed autocorrelation
is high at short lag. Carrying forward an already-extreme observation is a
strong strategy precisely while an episode continues, and it requires no
willingness to extrapolate. This is why the reference benchmark, weak in
absolute terms at long lead, still holds the severe endpoint.

What the secondary model contributes. GRU_R1 reduced severe MAE relative to
B3_R2 on the locked final test, at 109.33 against 131.90, and it retained more
severe detection recall at long lead. Its overall macro MAE is worse, at 33.08.
It is therefore a severe-magnitude improvement over the primary model and not a
replacement for it, and it did not improve on the reference benchmark in the
tail.

Why severe seed stability matters. Severe-tail metrics moved by up to 35
µg/m³ across five seeds while overall metrics moved by around one. Any
severe-tail conclusion resting on a single training run is fragile, and the
evaluated GRU artifact is a single seed. This is a limitation of the
confirmatory design itself, not only of the analysis.

What the cross-station evidence supports. Under a paired contrast holding
everything but information access fixed, cross-station context improved the
same architecture in 5 of 5 seeds on development validation, most consistently
at 6 and 12 hours. That is a statement about information value.

What the attention analysis does not support. It does not support a
geographic interpretation. Attention weights show no proximity relationship —
Spearman +0.136289 against inter-station distance — even though distance and
observed correlation are strongly related in the same data. Whatever the
cross-station channel is exploiting, the diagnostics give no support for
reading it as physical transport between neighbouring sites.

What the foundation-model exclusion teaches. Two independent eligibility
constraints removed all three candidates before any inference: a
pretraining-overlap risk identified by provenance audit, and weight licensing.
Neither is a statement about forecasting quality. The lesson is that for
pretrained models, corpus provenance is part of evaluation design, and it has
to be settled before a checkpoint is downloaded rather than reported afterwards
as a caveat.

How this should influence future work. The gap this study measures is not
between model families; four learned configurations landed within 0.8 µg/m³ of
each other on development validation. The gap is between average-case and
extreme-case behaviour, and it was not closed by added architectural capacity.
Progress on severe episodes will more plausibly come from changing the
objective, the output representation or the evaluation target than from a
larger sequence model trained under the same absolute-error criterion.


12. Limitations

The following limitations are stated in full rather than summarised, because
several of them bound the study's central claims directly.

1. Single metropolitan network. One city, one pollutant, one monitoring
   network. Nothing here establishes behaviour in other urban environments.
2. No external-city validation. There is no independent external-city test
   set, so geographic generalisation is untested.
3. Historical, not forecast, meteorology. Only historical observed
   meteorology was available. No future weather forecast was used as an input,
   by design, so this study does not measure what a forecast-conditioned system
   would achieve.
4. The secondary artifact is a single seed. The evaluated GRU artifact is
   seed 42, while its secondary role was motivated by five-seed robustness
   evidence, and severe-tail metrics are the least seed-stable quantities
   measured.
5. The hypotheses were not all re-tested confirmatorily. H1 to H4 were not
   all independently re-tested on the locked final test, because the
   confirmatory set contained three models and no paired regime variant. Their
   status remains development-supported.
6. The severe threshold is study-internal. It is the training pooled P95,
   not a regulatory or air-quality-index boundary, and severe rates are not
   comparable to studies using regulatory definitions.
7. The error analysis is exploratory. The event, seasonal, detection and
   residual analyses are post-test exploratory and were not predeclared.
8. No predeclared uncertainty quantification. No formal inferential
   confidence intervals were predeclared for the locked test, so none is
   reported and no statement about sampling variability of the locked values is
   made.
9. LOSO scope. The transfer experiment measures
   held-out target-station supervision transfer with causal historical observations from the held-out station available.
   It does not measure forecasting at a site whose observation stream is
   absent.
10. Coordinates are analysis-only. Coordinates were used for descriptive
    spatial diagnostics only, never as a model feature, and were acquired after
    every model was frozen.
11. Attention is not geographic. SA_R3 attention was not proximity-driven
    and must not be interpreted as physical pollution transport.
12. No foundation model was evaluated. No eligible foundation model could
    be evaluated under the study's pretraining-overlap and licensing
    constraints, so this study offers no evidence about their performance here.
13. Negative predictions were left unclipped. Both neural heads are
    unconstrained; negative outputs are reported rather than clipped, which
    keeps the property visible but means the raw outputs are not directly
    usable as concentrations.
14. Residual dependence remains. Substantial short-lag residual
    autocorrelation remains in the locked predictions, so the models have not
    extracted all available temporal structure.
15. Severe forecasting is unresolved. Severe-episode forecasting remains an
    open problem in this study, and the reference benchmark was the strongest
    severe performer.
16. Operational usability was never defined. Operational early-warning
    usability was never formally defined or tested, so this study supports no
    conclusion in either direction about deployment.
17. Bitwise reproduction of the neural artifact requires care. GRU
    predictions carry an order-1e-5 dependence on inference batch composition
    arising from floating-point reduction order. This is immaterial at the
    precision reported, but exact bitwise reproduction requires the identical
    batch structure.


13. Future work

Every direction below is motivated by an observed failure mode of this study.
None was tested in this study, and no claim is made that any of them
improves on the results reported here.

1. Tail-aware loss functions. Not tested in this study.
2. Probabilistic forecasting. Not tested in this study.
3. Quantile forecasting for the upper tail. Not tested in this study.
4. Event-aware training objectives. Not tested in this study.
5. Persistence/model hybrid gating, motivated by the reference benchmark's tail
   behaviour and by the measured error complementarity. *Not tested in this
   study.*
6. Forecast meteorology as an exogenous input. Not tested in this study.
7. Cross-city external validation. Not tested in this study.
8. A clean-pretraining foundation-model benchmark. Not tested in this study.
9. Graph or geographic inductive biases. Not tested in this study.
10. Calibrated severe-event warning probabilities, framed as classification
    rather than point regression. Not tested in this study.
11. Longer contexts where scientifically justified. Not tested in this study.
12. Uncertainty quantification with predeclared temporal block methods. *Not
    tested in this study.*


14. Conclusion

On a sealed test block covering 2016-03-01 to 2017-02-28, opened exactly once
under a hierarchy frozen beforehand, a gradient-boosted model over causal
pollutant, meteorological and temporal history achieved a macro station-horizon
MAE of 31.99 µg/m³ against 36.14 for causal persistence — an improvement of
11.49% — and improved on persistence at every horizon from 1 to 24 hours. In
the severe stratum the prespecified secondary model reached 109.33 µg/m³
against 131.90 for the primary model, while persistence remained the strongest
severe reference benchmark at 93.22.

Development and robustness evidence showed substantial value from target PM2.5
history, weak and model-dependent value from co-pollutant history, and a paired
cross-station benefit favouring the cross-station regime in all five seeds.
None of those contrasts was independently re-tested on the locked final test.

Post-test exploratory analysis is consistent with regression toward the
conditional mean during extreme episodes, and substantial short-lag residual
dependence remains in the locked predictions.

Average multi-horizon accuracy improved measurably. Extreme-episode accuracy
did not. The principal open problem is reliable forecasting of extreme
pollution episodes rather than average multi-horizon accuracy, and on that
endpoint a one-line causal rule remained the strongest benchmark in this study.
